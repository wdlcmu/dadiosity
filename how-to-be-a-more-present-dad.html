How to be a more present dad

You walk in the door. She runs to you before your bag hits the floor. Being present is that moment, done on purpose. Greet her first when you get home. Keep your phone off the table. Stop for what she finds on a walk.

Dadiosity Daily, Lesson 21: Make Hello a Big Deal

Make hello a big deal

The idea: How you greet your kid sets the note when you meet again. Bags, phone, and small talk can come first by habit. She watches where she lands in that minute.

Try this tonight: At the next homecoming, greet her first. Put bags down, put the phone away, get low, and give her a hug and her name with joy. Let the rest wait a minute.

Tonight's play: Make up a hello you both use each time. A high five, a spin, a nose tap, in the same order. Practice it three times tonight till you both know it well.

Why it works: A big hello tells her she comes first in your day. That warm minute can lift the whole night. She will run to a dad who is glad to see her.

One line to keep: Greet her first when you reunite, before bags, phone, or other talk.

Dadiosity Daily, Lesson 26: Phones Off the Table

Phones off the table

The idea: A phone on the table pulls your eyes, when it stays dark. Your kid can feel that pull in the middle of a meal. She competes with a thing she cannot beat.

Try this tonight: Tonight, put your phone in a room far from the table before dinner starts. Leave it there for the meal. Look at her when she talks, and let calls wait till plates clear.

Tonight's play: After dinner, play a look close game with a tray of things. She studies the tray, you hide one thing, and she names what is gone. Swap roles and play two rounds.

Why it works: Full eyes at a meal tell her she is worth your whole mind. Talk grows when no screen cuts in. She learns meals are for people at the table.

One line to keep: Keep your phone in another room for the whole meal.

Dadiosity Daily, Lesson 14: Stop for What She Finds

Stop for what she finds

The idea: On a walk, your kid sees a world you walk past. A leaf, a bug, a stone. When you rush past her finds, she learns her wonder is in the way.

Try this tonight: Tonight, on a short walk, stop for what she finds. Crouch down and look with her for a minute. Ask one thing about it. Then let her lead the look before you walk on.

Tonight's play: Tonight at home, hide three soft toys in one room. Hunt for them slow, one by one. When she finds one, stop and cheer the find like it is gold.

Why it works: Time you give to her wonder tells her she matters more than the clock. She learns to look close at the world. You get to see it through her eyes.

One line to keep: Stop for one thing she finds on a walk and look at it with her for a minute.

Two more small ones

Start the morning slow. The first minutes set the tone for the home. Lay out clothes and cups the night before. When she wakes, sit with her for five minutes. Hold her, talk soft, and ask for nothing yet.

Tell her a story from when you were small. Keep it short and true, with a feeling in it. Name how you felt, what went wrong, and who helped you feel safe again.
