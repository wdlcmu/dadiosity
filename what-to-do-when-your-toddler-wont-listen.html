What to do when your toddler won't listen

You ask her to come to the table. She keeps playing, like you never spoke. Ask one time, then pause. Count slow to ten in your head before you say it again, in the same words. If she falls apart, check the basics first: hungry, thirsty, tired, or needs the bathroom. Most hard moments are one of those, not a fight with you.

Dadiosity Daily, Lesson 9: Pause After You Ask

Ask once, then pause

The idea: Little kids need time to hear an ask and act on it. Their minds move at a kind pace, not a grown-up pace. When you repeat, the words pile up and get lost.

Try this tonight: Tonight, ask one time, then pause. Count slow to ten in your head. Keep your face calm and your hands still. If she has not moved, say the same words once more, just as plain.

Tonight's play: Play a freeze game with music or claps. Move, then freeze when the sound stops. Take turns starting and stopping the sound. Laugh when you freeze mid step.

Why it works: Quiet time lets her mind catch up to your words. She feels less rushed, and you feel less mad. One calm ask beats five loud ones, most nights.

One line to keep: Ask once, then pause while you count slow to ten before you say it again.

Dadiosity Daily, Lesson 24: Check the Body First

Check the body first

The idea: A melt down is not always a mood. A small body can be hungry, thirsty, tired, or in need of a bathroom break. Those needs can sound like anger or tears.

Try this tonight: Tonight, when she falls apart, check her body before you judge her mood. Offer water, a bite, a bathroom trip, or a rest. Keep your voice kind while you check each one.

Tonight's play: Play a body check game with a stuffed bear. Pretend the bear is cranky, then guess what it needs. Give it water, food, or a nap, and cheer when it feels well.

Why it works: A met need can end a storm in a plain way. She learns to read her body signs over time. You skip a talk when a snack was the real fix.

One line to keep: Check her body for hunger, thirst, tiredness, or a bathroom need before you judge her mood.

Dadiosity Daily, Lesson 18: Bring Your Voice Down First

Bring your voice down first

The idea: When the room gets loud, your kid gets loud right back. She matches the sound she hears. A big voice from you makes her big voice grow even bigger.

Try this tonight: Tonight, when noise climbs, drop your voice before you ask her to drop hers. Speak soft and slow, and keep your words short. Let quiet be what you hand her.

Tonight's play: Play a soft voice game. Whisper a word for her to guess, then let her whisper one for you. Go back and forth, hushed and giggling, till words run out.

Why it works: A soft voice is hard to fight with. She leans in to hear you, and leaning in calms her. You set the sound of the room by the sound you bring to it each night.

One line to keep: Drop your own voice to soft and slow before you ask her to quiet down.

Four more things that help

Name the feeling first. When feelings get big, she has no words for them yet. Name it before you fix it. Say, you are mad. You wanted more. Then wait a beat before you help.

Say no once, and stay close. Say no one time, short and kind. No more sweets tonight. Then stay near her and let the sad pass. She learns the line will hold, and love holds too.

When she is stuck on one want, show her something new. Point to a new thing and name it with wonder. A red leaf. A loud truck. Put a safe small thing in her hands. The no still holds, but her mind has moved on.

Before the next hard part, check the basics first. Hunger, a tired body, and a long wait add up. Before a trip out, check food, rest, bathroom, and what comes next. Fix the one need you can fix, then name the plan in one short line.
