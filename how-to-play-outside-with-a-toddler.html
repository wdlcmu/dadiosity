How to play outside with a toddler

Let the walk be the play. Stop for what she finds, and look at it with her for a full minute. Play find it with colors and shapes as you go. Let her pick one small thing, a leaf or a stone, to make a gift for someone back home.

Dadiosity Daily, Lesson 14: Stop for What She Finds

Stop for what she finds

The idea: On a walk, your kid sees a world you walk past. A leaf, a bug, a stone. When you rush past her finds, she learns her wonder is in the way.

Try this tonight: Tonight, on a short walk, stop for what she finds. Crouch down and look with her for a minute. Ask one thing about it. Then let her lead the look before you walk on.

Tonight's play: Tonight at home, hide three soft toys in one room. Hunt for them slow, one by one. When she finds one, stop and cheer the find like it is gold.

Why it works: Time you give to her wonder tells her she matters more than the clock. She learns to look close at the world. You get to see it through her eyes.

One line to keep: Stop for one thing she finds on a walk and look at it with her for a minute.

Dadiosity Daily, Lesson 30: Turn Her Eyes to a New Thing

Play find it

The idea: When a kid is stuck on one want, more words can keep her stuck. Her eyes lead her mind. A new thing to see or hold can break the loop.

Try this tonight: Tonight, when she is stuck and tears rise, do not repeat the no. Point to a new thing and name it with wonder. A red leaf. A loud truck. Put a safe small thing in her hands.

Tonight's play: Play a find it game at home. Call out a color or a shape and hunt for it together. Cheer each find, then call the next one fast.

Why it works: A new sight gives her mind a new job. Hands that hold a thing have less work for a fight. The no still holds, but her mind has moved on.

One line to keep: When she is stuck, turn her eyes and hands to a new thing instead of repeating the no.

Dadiosity Daily, Lesson 27: Make a Gift for Someone

Make a gift for someone

The idea: Your kid gets a lot of gifts, help, and care each day. Making a thing for someone turns that flow around. Giving lets her feel the joy on the other side.

Try this tonight: Tonight, help her make one small gift for a real person. A drawn card, a painted stone, a leaf she picked. Ask who it is for, then let her lead the making.

Tonight's play: Set up a small gift shop of her drawings and crafts. She wraps one pick in cloth or paper for its new owner. You help tie it, then plan the giving.

Why it works: Making for others grows care she can feel in her hands. She sees her work bring a smile to a face. Pride from giving lasts longer than pride from getting things.

One line to keep: Help her make one small gift for a real person, then give it to that person.

Four more moves, in short

Follow her lead out there too. Let her pick the game and the path for ten minutes. Do not add rules or fix the plot. Ask what happens next, then do that.

Say what you see. Plain talk during small moments does the word work. Name things in short lines as you walk. Wet grass. Loud bird. Big stone. Pause after each line so she can try a word back to you.

Run the buzz off. A small body holds a lot of buzz, and outside is the best place to burn it. Jump, spin, march, and shake with her for two minutes.

Catch the good out loud. Watch for one good thing she does and name it at once, in plain detail. You carried your stick the whole way.
