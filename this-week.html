Monday, Nov 9

Dadiosity Daily · Lesson 43

Bath Is a Place, Not a Step

The idea: Bath fails when it is a chore between dinner and bed. As a place with its own fun, it pulls her in instead of needing a push.

Try this tonight: Start the water and set out the boats before you call her. Let her climb in for the play. The washing rides along in the middle, quick and plain. Warm water, her towel waiting, pajamas laid out on the bed.

Tonight's play: Tonight the bath is boat testing. Which cups float, which spoons sink, which toy swims best. She runs every test.

Why it works: A thing she runs toward needs no fight to start. Clean happens inside the fun, not instead of it. And by the time she climbs in, bedtime is already half done.

One line to keep: Start the water and the boats before you call her, and let the washing ride along inside the play.

Tuesday, Nov 10

Dadiosity Daily · Lesson 44

The Table Has a Beginning and an End

The idea: A toddler meal is short, and that is fine. The chair war starts when the meal has no clear start and no clear end.

Try this tonight: Start the meal together, at the table, food already on it. When she says she is done, believe her the second time. The food stays, she may go, the meal is over. No bites counted, no deals made.

Tonight's play: Play the dinner bell game. She sets the table, rings a spoon on a cup, and everyone sits. Two rings, and the meal is done.

Why it works: Her job is to decide if and how much she eats. Your job is the food, the time, and the place. Clear jobs end most table fights.

One line to keep: You own the food, the time, and the place; she owns whether and how much; done means done.

Wednesday, Nov 11

Dadiosity Daily · Lesson 45

Give the Car Ride a Job

The idea: Strapped in, bored, and facing the back of a seat: a car seat is a hard place to be good. A job gives the ride edges.

Try this tonight: Before the car moves, give her the spotter job. Count buses. Call out every dog. One short story from you per ride, started as the wheels start. A short ride gets a short job; save the long story for the highway.

Tonight's play: Play pretend drive at home. Chairs in a row, she calls the stops, and you both buckle the stuffed passengers in tight.

Why it works: Eyes with a job scan the world instead of the straps. A story that starts at wheels up makes the ride a thing with a shape.

One line to keep: Give her the spotter job before the car moves, and start one short story as the wheels start.

Thursday, Nov 12

Dadiosity Daily · Lesson 1

Get Down Low

The idea: Your kid hears you best when you are close. Standing tall makes your voice feel far away. Getting low makes it near and calm. Small kids read your face more than your words.

Try this tonight: Tonight, pick one moment at home, like greeting time or dinner time. Get down on one knee or sit on the floor. Come to her eye level. Say her name, wait for her eyes, then speak.

Tonight's play: Sit on the floor with a soft ball. Roll it to her and wait. When she rolls it back, cheer like it is a big win. Let the game stay slow and silly.

Why it works: A close face feels safe, and safe kids listen better. Eye level also slows you down. That calm is the first lesson she copies from you.

One line to keep: Get to eye level before you speak.

Friday, Nov 13

Dadiosity Daily · Lesson 2

Name It First

The idea: When feelings get big, your kid has no words for them yet. If you name the feeling first, she does not have to shout to be known.

Try this tonight: At dinner or bath time, when she gets upset, name it before you fix it. Say, you are mad. You wanted more. Then wait a beat before you help.

Tonight's play: Stand at a mirror together. Make a happy face, a sad face, and a mad face. Name each one out loud. Let her pick the next face for you to make.

Why it works: Words give a feeling a smaller shape. A named feeling is easier to hold. She learns the words by hearing them at the moment she needs them most.

One line to keep: Name the feeling before you fix the problem.

Saturday, Nov 14

Dadiosity Daily · Lesson 3

Warn Her Before It Ends

The idea: Hard moments often come at a switch. Play stops, dinner starts, or the bath ends. A sudden switch can feel like a loss. A fair warning makes it easier.

Try this tonight: Tonight, warn her two times before each switch. Say, two more minutes, then we wash hands. Say it once more at one minute. Then follow through, calm and kind.

Tonight's play: Practice the switch as a game. Send a stuffed bear down a slide made of books. Count down out loud together. Clap when the bear lands and the turn ends.

Why it works: A warning gives her mind time to move. Time to move means fewer tears at the edge. She trusts the switch when it is not a surprise.

One line to keep: Warn two times before a change, then follow through.

Sunday, Nov 15

Dadiosity Daily · Lesson 4

Say What You See

The idea: Your kid learns words from your voice all day long. You do not need a special lesson time for that. Plain talk during small moments does the work.

Try this tonight: At bath time tonight, say what you see in short lines. Warm water. Blue cup. Wet toes. Pause after each line so she can try a word back to you.

Tonight's play: Stack blocks together and talk through each one. Red block on top. Big block falls down. Laugh, build it again, and keep the words just as plain as that.

Why it works: Kids copy words they hear at the moment the words fit. Short lines are easy to grab and try. Your calm voice turns plain time into word time.

One line to keep: Narrate small moments in short, plain lines.
