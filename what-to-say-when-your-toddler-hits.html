What to say when your toddler hits

Get low, and keep it to one short line. Hitting hurts. I will not let you hit. Then name what she feels: you are mad. You wanted the truck. Stay close while the feeling passes. Later, catch her gentle hands out loud, so gentle is what gets named. Three full lessons below, then the short versions of four more.

Dadiosity Daily, Lesson 1: Get Down Low

Get down low first

The idea: Your kid hears you best when you are close. Standing tall makes your voice feel far away. Getting low makes it near and calm. Small kids read your face more than your words.

Tonight's move: Tonight, pick one moment at home, like greeting time or dinner time. Get down on one knee or sit on the floor. Come to her eye level. Say her name, wait for her eyes, then speak.

Tonight's play: Sit on the floor with a soft ball. Roll it to her and wait. When she rolls it back, cheer like it is a big win. Let the game stay slow and silly.

Why it works: A close face feels safe, and safe kids listen better. Eye level also slows you down. That calm is the first lesson she copies from you.

Core move: Get to eye level before you speak.

Dadiosity Daily, Lesson 11: Hold the Line, Stay Close

Say it once, and stay close

The idea: A limit is not a fight. You can say no and be warm. Walking off or long talks can make a small no feel big and cold. Calm and close keeps it small.

Tonight's move: Tonight, if you say no, say it once, short and kind. No more sweets tonight. Then stay near her. Sit, breathe, and let the sad pass. Do not add threats or a talk.

Tonight's play: Build a tower and let it fall. Build it back the same way, block by block. Show her a fall is not the end of the fun or the game.

Why it works: She learns the line will hold, and love holds too. Both truths feel safe. A dad who stays close is a dad she can trust with hard news.

Core move: Say no once, short and kind, then stay close while the feelings pass.

Dadiosity Daily, Lesson 17: Catch the Good Out Loud

Catch gentle hands out loud

The idea: Your kid hears a lot of no and stop in a day. Good things she does can pass with no words at all. What you name out loud is what she will repeat.

Tonight's move: Tonight, watch for one good thing she does and name it at once. Say what she did, in plain detail. You put your cup in the sink. That helped us.

Tonight's play: Play a kind spy game at home. Each of you hunts for a good deed to name out loud. She finds one of yours, you find one of hers, and you cheer each find.

Why it works: A named good deed feels like a win she can hold. She will chase that warm feeling again. You train your own eyes too, to see the good first.

Core move: Name one good thing she does, in detail, right when she does it.

Four more moves, in short

Name the feeling behind the hit. When feelings get big, she has no words for them yet. Name it before you fix it. Say, you are mad. You wanted more. Words give a feeling a smaller shape, and a named feeling is easier to hold.

Check her body too. Hitting can ride on a tired or hungry body. Check hunger, thirst, tiredness, and a bathroom need before you judge her mood. A met need can end a storm in a plain way.

Say it soft. She matches the sound she hears. Drop your voice to soft and slow, and keep your words short. A soft voice is hard to fight with, and leaning in to hear you calms her.

Breathe out slow with her. When feelings rise fast, words can land too soon. Get low and blow out long, like cooling soup. Count each out breath on your fingers. Do three rounds before you use words.
