Dadiosity Daily, Lesson 20: Shake It Out Before You Sit

Shake it out before you sit

The idea: A small body holds a lot of buzz by dinner time. Sitting still is hard when that buzz has no way out. Asking for calm while her body hums sets you both up to fail.

Tonight's move: Tonight, before dinner, take two minutes to move with her. Jump, spin, march, shake each arm and leg out. End slow, with three deep breaths, then walk to the table.

Tonight's play: Put on one fast song and dance like the floor is hot. Freeze when it stops, fall down laughing, then get up and go again till the song ends.

Why it works: Big moves burn off the buzz that makes sitting hard. Her body settles when it has done its work. You get a calm meal start. She gets to wiggle first.

Core move: Move and shake with her for two minutes before you ask her to sit.

Dadiosity Daily, Lesson 26: Phones Off the Table

Phones off the table

The idea: A phone on the table pulls your eyes, when it stays dark. Your kid can feel that pull in the middle of a meal. She competes with a thing she cannot beat.

Tonight's move: Tonight, put your phone in a room far from the table before dinner starts. Leave it there for the meal. Look at her when she talks, and let calls wait till plates clear.

Tonight's play: After dinner, play a look close game with a tray of things. She studies the tray, you hide one thing, and she names what is gone. Swap roles and play two rounds.

Why it works: Full eyes at a meal tell her she is worth your whole mind. Talk grows when no screen cuts in. She learns meals are for people at the table.

Core move: Keep your phone in another room for the whole meal.

Dadiosity Daily, Lesson 29: One New Bite Beside a Safe Food

One new bite beside a safe food

The idea: New foods can feel like a risk. If the whole plate is new, she may eat none of it. One small new bite beside food she trusts feels safe.

Tonight's move: Tonight, put one small bite of a new food beside a food she likes. Name it plain, then say no more about it. Let her touch it, smell it, or leave it. Praise a try, not a clean plate.

Tonight's play: Make a tasting plate for a stuffed bear. One food it loves, one new food to sniff. Let her serve the bear first, then set hers like it.

Why it works: A safe food keeps the meal calm. A small new bite is a low risk she can say yes to. Tries add up over time, no fight.

Core move: Put one small new bite beside a food she trusts, with no push to finish it.

Four more moves, in short

Check her body first. A hard time at the table is not always a mood. She may be hungry, thirsty, tired, or in need of a bathroom break. Check each one before you judge the mood.

Catch the good out loud at the table. Watch for one good thing she does and name it at once, in plain detail. You put your cup in the sink. That helped us.

Say what you see at the meal. Plain talk during small moments does the word work. Say what you see in short lines, and pause after each line so she can try a word back to you.

Tidy to one song after the meal. A big mess can feel too big for a small kid. Put toys in one basket till one short song ends, then stop, even if a few toys stay out.
