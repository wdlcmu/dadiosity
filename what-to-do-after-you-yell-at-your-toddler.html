What to do after you yell at your toddler

Dadiosity Daily, Lesson 5: Fix It Fast

Fix it fast

The idea: You will snap some days. Every dad does. What lasts is not the snap on its own. What lasts is how fast you come back and make it right.

Tonight's move: If you raise your voice tonight, stop as soon as you can. Take one slow breath. Get low, say you are sorry, and name what you did. Then hug or sit close.

Tonight's play: Pretend to bump a stuffed bear by mistake. Say sorry to the bear together. Give it a hug and a bandage made of cloth. Let her lead the care.

Why it works: A quick fix shows her that love comes back after a hard moment. She learns that a bad moment can still end well. That lesson shapes how she fixes things too.

Core move: After you snap, stop, say sorry, and reconnect fast.

Dadiosity Daily, Lesson 23: Breathe Out Slow With Her

Breathe out slow with her

The idea: When feelings rise fast, words can land too soon. Her chest is tight, her face is hot, and talk feels like noise. Breath gives you a first step that needs no talk.

Tonight's move: Tonight, when she gets upset, get low and breathe out slow with her. Blow out long, like cooling soup. Count each out breath on your fingers. Do three rounds before you use words.

Tonight's play: Blow a light scarf or tissue across the floor with long breaths. Take turns moving it one step at a time. Laugh at the hops, then slow each blow down.

Why it works: A long out breath helps a body let go. Doing it with you makes it safe to copy. She learns a way to calm that lives in her own body.

Core move: Breathe out slow with her for three rounds before you use words.

Dadiosity Daily, Lesson 1: Get Down Low

Get down low

The idea: Your kid hears you best when you are close. Standing tall makes your voice feel far away. Getting low makes it near and calm. Small kids read your face more than your words.

Tonight's move: Tonight, pick one moment at home, like greeting time or dinner time. Get down on one knee or sit on the floor. Come to her eye level. Say her name, wait for her eyes, then speak.

Tonight's play: Sit on the floor with a soft ball. Roll it to her and wait. When she rolls it back, cheer like it is a big win. Let the game stay slow and silly.

Why it works: A close face feels safe, and safe kids listen better. Eye level also slows you down. That calm is the first lesson she copies from you.

Core move: Get to eye level before you speak.

Four more moves, in short

Bring your voice down first. She matches the sound she hears. Drop your voice before you ask her to drop hers. Speak soft and slow, and keep your words short. Let quiet be what you hand her.

Name her feeling, and yours. When feelings get big, she has no words for them yet. Name the feeling first. Say, you are mad. You wanted more. Then wait a beat before you help. Words give a feeling a smaller shape.

Stay close after you say it. A limit is not a fight, and a sorry is not a walk off. Say what you need to say once, short and kind, then stay near her. Sit, breathe, and let the sad pass.

Let her finish first. She tells things in a slow way, and stops and starts. Let her get to the end before you reply. Keep your eyes on her and nod. Being heard all the way out tells her her words count.

What do I do after I yell at my toddler?
