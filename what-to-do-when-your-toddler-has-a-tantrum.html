What to do when your toddler has a tantrum

Stay close, and keep your part small. Name the feeling before you fix anything. Breathe out slow with her, three rounds, before you use words. Say your no one time, short and kind, then stay near while the storm passes.

Dadiosity Daily, Lesson 2: Name It First

Name the feeling first

The idea: When feelings get big, your kid has no words for them yet. If you name the feeling first, she does not have to shout to be known.

Tonight's move: At dinner or bath time, when she gets upset, name it before you fix it. Say, you are mad. You wanted more. Then wait a beat before you help.

Tonight's play: Stand at a mirror together. Make a happy face, a sad face, and a mad face. Name each one out loud. Let her pick the next face for you to make.

Why it works: Words give a feeling a smaller shape. A named feeling is easier to hold. She learns the words by hearing them at the moment she needs them most.

Core move: Name the feeling before you fix the problem.

Dadiosity Daily, Lesson 23: Breathe Out Slow With Her

Breathe out slow with her

The idea: When feelings rise fast, words can land too soon. Her chest is tight, her face is hot, and talk feels like noise. Breath gives you a first step that needs no talk.

Tonight's move: Tonight, when she gets upset, get low and breathe out slow with her. Blow out long, like cooling soup. Count each out breath on your fingers. Do three rounds before you use words.

Tonight's play: Blow a light scarf or tissue across the floor with long breaths. Take turns moving it one step at a time. Laugh at the hops, then slow each blow down.

Why it works: A long out breath helps a body let go. Doing it with you makes it safe to copy. She learns a way to calm that lives in her own body.

Core move: Breathe out slow with her for three rounds before you use words.

Dadiosity Daily, Lesson 11: Hold the Line, Stay Close

Say no once, and stay close

The idea: A limit is not a fight. You can say no and be warm. Walking off or long talks can make a small no feel big and cold. Calm and close keeps it small.

Tonight's move: Tonight, if you say no, say it once, short and kind. No more sweets tonight. Then stay near her. Sit, breathe, and let the sad pass. Do not add threats or a talk.

Tonight's play: Build a tower and let it fall. Build it back the same way, block by block. Show her a fall is not the end of the fun or the game.

Why it works: She learns the line will hold, and love holds too. Both truths feel safe. A dad who stays close is a dad she can trust with hard news.

Core move: Say no once, short and kind, then stay close while the feelings pass.

Four more moves, in short

Check her body first. A melt down is not always a mood. She may be hungry, thirsty, tired, or in need of a bathroom break. Offer water, a bite, a trip, or a rest before you judge the mood.

Get down low. Your kid hears you best when you are close. Get down on one knee or sit on the floor. Come to her eye level, say her name, and wait for her eyes.

After the storm, shake it out. A small body holds a lot of buzz. When the tears slow down, take two minutes to move with her. Jump, spin, march, shake each arm and leg out. End slow, with three deep breaths.

If you snap, fix it fast. You will snap some days. Stop as soon as you can. Take one slow breath, get low, and say you are sorry. Name what you did, then hug or sit close.
