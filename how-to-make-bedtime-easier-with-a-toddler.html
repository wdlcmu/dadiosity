How to make bedtime easier with a toddler

It is 7:30. Bath, book, song. She knows what comes next, so she settles. Keep the same three steps in the same order each night. A night she can predict feels safe, and safe kids settle with less fight.

Before the routine starts: warn her

Hard moments often come at a change, and bedtime is the biggest change of the day. Warn her two times before it starts. Say, two more minutes, then we wash hands. Say it once more at one minute. Then follow through, calm and kind. A warning gives her mind time to move, and she trusts the change when it is not a surprise.

Dadiosity Daily · Lesson 6

Same Steps at Bedtime

The idea: Bedtime goes better when the end of the day feels known. Same steps, in the same order, tell her body what comes next. New tricks each night do the opposite.

Try this tonight: Pick three steps tonight and keep the order fixed. Bath, book, then song works well. Say each step as you start it. Do not add a fourth step when she asks for more.

Tonight's play: Put a stuffed bear to bed before her turn comes. A bath in a toy tub, one short book, one short song. Use the same order you will use for her.

Why it works: A known order feels safe at night. Safe kids settle with less fight in them. She can rest because she knows the road home to sleep.

One line to keep: Keep the same three bedtime steps in the same order.

Dadiosity Daily · Lesson 7

End on One Good Thing

The idea: The last words of the day tend to stick. If the day ends on a hard note, it can color the whole day. One good moment, named out loud, can hold the day well.

Try this tonight: At bedtime tonight, name one good moment from the day. Keep it small and true. Say yours first, then ask for hers. Take any answer she gives, even a short one.

Tonight's play: Act out the good moment with two stuffed animals. Keep it short and a bit silly. Let her pick which animal plays her part in it. End the show with a cheer.

Why it works: Naming the good trains the mind to go and find it. Kids sleep well after a warm last thought. You will carry that same good moment into your night too.

One line to keep: Name one true good moment as the last thing at bedtime.

Dadiosity Daily · Lesson 28

Pick One Thing for Tomorrow

The idea: A day can end with tired thoughts if you let it. What comes next can feel big and vague to a kid. One good thing, named, gives tomorrow a clear shape.

Try this tonight: Tonight at bedtime, ask her to pick one small thing to look forward to tomorrow. Pancakes, a park walk, a song in the car. Say it back plain, then let the night rest there.

Tonight's play: Draw tomorrow on a scrap of paper together. One sun, one star pick, one scene of her good thing. Stick it by her bed so morning starts with that plan.

Why it works: A named good thing gives hope a handle she can hold. She falls asleep facing forward, not stuck on today. Morning starts with a plan you both share and like.

One line to keep: At bedtime, let her pick one small thing to look forward to tomorrow.

When she will not settle

Get low and breathe out slow with her. Blow out long, like cooling soup. Count each out breath on your fingers. Do three rounds before you use words.

Quick question

How can I make bedtime easier with a toddler?

Keep the same three steps in the same order each night, like bath, book, then song, and do not add a fourth step. Warn her twice before bedtime starts, end by naming one good moment from the day, and let her pick one small thing to look forward to tomorrow.
