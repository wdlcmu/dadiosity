Things to do with a toddler at the grocery store

Halfway down the aisle, she wants out of the cart. Give her a job, and the store gets easier. She can carry the list, find the bananas, and put the cans in the cart. Say what you see as you go, in short lines. Let her pick one new food to try at home, next to a food she likes.

Dadiosity Daily, Lesson 10: Give Her a Real Job

Give her a real job

The idea: Little kids want to belong, not be helped. A real job tells her she counts in this home. Fake jobs fool no one. Small true jobs build pride fast.

Try this tonight: Tonight, give her one real job she can finish. Carry the napkins to the table. Put the spoons by each plate. Wipe one small spill with her cloth. Thank her for the work, by name.

Tonight's play: Set up a post office with paper and a box. She stamps each card and drops it in the box. You write one card to her bear and thank her for the mail.

Why it works: Work she can see done makes her feel able. Able kids try more and whine less for control. You are not just feeding her. You are raising a helper.

One line to keep: Give her one real job she can finish, one that truly helps the home.

Dadiosity Daily, Lesson 4: Say What You See

Say what you see

The idea: Your kid learns words from your voice all day long. You do not need a special lesson time for that. Plain talk during small moments does the work.

Try this tonight: At bath time tonight, say what you see in short lines. Warm water. Blue cup. Wet toes. Pause after each line so she can try a word back to you.

Tonight's play: Stack blocks together and talk through each one. Red block on top. Big block falls down. Laugh, build it again, and keep the words just as plain as that.

Why it works: Kids copy words they hear at the moment the words fit. Short lines are easy to grab and try. Your calm voice turns plain time into word time.

One line to keep: Narrate small moments in short, plain lines.

Dadiosity Daily, Lesson 29: One New Bite Beside a Safe Food

One new bite beside a safe food

The idea: New foods can feel like a risk. If the whole plate is new, she may eat none of it. One small new bite beside food she trusts feels safe.

Try this tonight: Tonight, put one small bite of a new food beside a food she likes. Name it plain, then say no more about it. Let her touch it, smell it, or leave it. Praise a try, not a clean plate.

Tonight's play: Make a tasting plate for a stuffed bear. One food it loves, one new food to sniff. Let her serve the bear first, then set hers like it.

Why it works: A safe food keeps the meal calm. A small new bite is a low risk she can say yes to. Tries add up over time, no fight.

One line to keep: Put one small new bite beside a food she trusts, with no push to finish it.

Four more things that help

Give two good choices in the aisle. Swap one order for two choices you can live with. Red apples or green apples. This box or that box. Then let her pick, and honor the pick.

Before you go in, check the basics first. Hunger, a tired body, and a long wait add up. Before the trip, check food, rest, and the bathroom. Fix the one need you can fix, then name the plan in one short line.

Say what she did right, out loud, right there. Name one good thing she does, in plain detail, right when she does it. You held the cart the whole aisle. That helped us.

When she is stuck on one want, show her something new. Point to a new thing and name it with wonder. A red pepper. A big fish. Put a safe small thing in her hands. The no still holds, but her mind has moved on.
