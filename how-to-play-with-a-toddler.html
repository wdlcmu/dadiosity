How to play with a toddler

Let her lead. For ten minutes, follow her play idea just as she gives it. Do not add rules or fix the plot. That one change does most of the work. Four full lessons below show the rest, each about a minute to read.

Dadiosity Daily, Lesson 22: Follow Her Lead in Play

Follow her lead

The idea: Most play has a grown up plan under it. You pick the game, set the rules, and fix what drifts. When she leads, you get to see how her mind works.

Tonight's move: Tonight, for ten minutes, follow her play idea just as she gives it. Do not add rules, fix the plot, or swap in a new game. Ask what happens next, then do that.

Tonight's play: Build a town from blocks and soft toys. She picks who lives there and where roads go. You move the cars where she sends them and say the lines she gives you.

Why it works: Led play tells her her ideas can hold a room. She thinks in steps and tries them out loud. You learn what she loves by staying inside her plan.

Core move: Follow her play idea for ten minutes without leading or fixing it.

Dadiosity Daily, Lesson 16: Hands Back, Let Her Try

Hands back, let her try

The idea: Your kid wants to do hard things on her own. A tight lid, a shoe, a zip. When you take it from her too soon, she learns to quit and wait for help.

Tonight's move: Tonight, when she works on a hard thing, keep your hands back for a full slow count. Watch close. Cheer the tries, not just the win. Help only if she asks.

Tonight's play: Put a few safe lids and jars on the floor. Let her open and close each one at her own pace. You hold the jar still. She does the turning.

Why it works: A hard thing she does on her own sticks with her for a long time. She feels strong in her own hands. Waiting shows trust she can feel and copy.

Core move: Keep your hands back and let her try a hard thing herself before you help.

Dadiosity Daily, Lesson 19: Let Her Teach You

Let her teach you

The idea: Most of the day, you show and she copies. That is fine, but it is one way. When she gets to teach you, she stands tall in a new way for you both.

Tonight's move: Tonight, ask her to teach you one small thing she knows well. A dance step, a song with claps, a way to hold a crayon. Try it her way, and let her fix you.

Tonight's play: Swap seats for school play at home. She is the teacher, you are the kid at the desk. Raise your hand, try hard, and let her praise your good work.

Why it works: Teaching makes her sort what she knows into clear steps. Pride grows when dad learns from her. You show her learning is for all ages, not just kids.

Core move: Ask her to teach you one small thing, then learn it her way.

Dadiosity Daily, Lesson 15: Let Her Finish First

Let her finish first

The idea: Your kid tells things in a slow way. She stops, starts, and adds bits out of order. If you jump in to finish for her, she may stop trying to tell you.

Tonight's move: Tonight, when she tells you a story, let her get to the end. Keep your eyes on her and nod. Hold your words back, even when you know what comes next.

Tonight's play: Let her tell you the plot of a book you both know. Sit back and hear the whole tale. Ask one thing at the end about a part she liked best.

Why it works: Being heard all the way out tells her her words count. She talks more when she feels heard. You learn more about her day than a quick guess can give you.

Core move: Let her finish her whole story before you reply, with no jump in.
