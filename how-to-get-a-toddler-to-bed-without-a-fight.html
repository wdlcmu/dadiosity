How to get a toddler to bed without a fight

It is 7 p.m. You say bedtime, and she runs the other way. The fight starts before bedtime. Warn her two times before play ends, so bed is not a surprise. Give her choices inside the steps: which pajamas, which book. Bed itself is not a choice. End with one short story from when you were small.

Dadiosity Daily, Lesson 3: Warn Her Before It Ends

Warn her before it ends

The idea: Hard moments often come at a switch. Play stops, dinner starts, or the bath ends. A sudden switch can feel like a loss. A fair warning makes it easier.

Try this tonight: Tonight, warn her two times before each change. Say, two more minutes, then we wash hands. Say it once more at one minute. Then follow through, calm and kind.

Tonight's play: Practice the change as a game. Send a stuffed bear down a slide made of books. Count down out loud together. Clap when the bear lands and the turn ends.

Why it works: A warning gives her mind time to move. Time to get ready means fewer tears at bedtime. She trusts the change when it is not a surprise.

One line to keep: Warn two times before a change, then follow through.

Dadiosity Daily, Lesson 8: Give Two Good Choices

Give two good choices

The idea: Little kids want a say in their day. When you give an order, she hears no room to move. Two good choices give her a say and keep you in charge.

Try this tonight: Tonight, swap one order for two choices you can live with. Say, red cup or blue cup. Say, wash hands now or carry the soap to the sink. Then let her pick and honor the pick.

Tonight's play: Line up two hats or two scarves. Let her pick one for you and one for her. Wear the pick with pride. Swap roles and pick for her stuffed bear.

Why it works: A choice is power she does not have to fight for. She practices picking, and you keep the edges safe. Small picks now build calm picks later.

One line to keep: Offer two good choices you can live with, then honor the pick she makes.

Dadiosity Daily, Lesson 25: When You Were Small

Tell a story from when you were small

The idea: Your kid sees you as big and sure most days. She may not know you were once small and scared too. A true story from your young days can bring you close.

Try this tonight: Tonight, tell her one short true story from when you were her age. Keep it small and plain. Name how you felt, what went wrong, and who helped you feel safe again.

Tonight's play: Act out your small story with two stuffed animals. One plays young you, one plays the helper. Keep it soft and short. Let her pick a happy line for the end.

Why it works: Hearing dad was small once makes her own fears feel less alone. She hears feelings pass and help comes. Stories like this build trust she will keep for years to come.

One line to keep: Tell her one short true story from when you were her age, with a feeling in it.

Four more things that help

Move first. Sitting still is hard when her body wants to move. Before the steps start, take two minutes to move with her. Jump, spin, march, shake it out. End slow, with three deep breaths, then begin.

Clean up while one song plays. Make the tidy the first step of the night. Put toys in one basket till one short song ends, then stop, even if a few toys stay out.

Bring your voice down as the night goes on. She matches the sound she hears. Speak soft and slow, and keep your words short as bed gets near.

Ask once, then pause. Little kids need time to hear an ask and act on it. Ask one time, then count slow to ten in your head before you say it again, in the same plain words.
