Dadiosity Daily, Lesson 13: Tidy to One Song

Tidy to one song

The idea: A big mess can feel too big for a small kid. She may quit before she starts. One short song cuts the job down to a size she can hold and finish.

Tonight's move: Tonight, pick one song you like. Put toys in one basket till the song ends. Stop when the song stops, even if a few toys stay out. Cheer for what got done, not what is left.

Tonight's play: Race toys back to their home box before the song ends. Take turns tossing one in from a short step away. End with a high five lap around.

Why it works: A clear end makes work feel fair. She can see the job shrink as she works. Short wins teach her that starting is the brave and doable part.

Core move: Tidy toys into one basket for the length of one song, then stop.

Dadiosity Daily, Lesson 10: Give Her a Real Job

Give her a real job

The idea: Little kids want to belong, not be helped. A real job tells her she counts in this home. Fake jobs fool no one. Small true jobs build pride fast.

Tonight's move: Tonight, give her one real job she can finish. Carry the napkins to the table. Put the spoons by each plate. Wipe one small spill with her cloth. Thank her for the work, by name.

Tonight's play: Set up a post office with paper and a box. She stamps each card and drops it in the box. You write one card to her bear and thank her for the mail.

Why it works: Work she can see done makes her feel able. Able kids try more and whine less for control. You are not just feeding her. You are raising a helper.

Core move: Give her one real job she can finish, one that truly helps the home.

Dadiosity Daily, Lesson 17: Catch the Good Out Loud

Catch the good out loud

The idea: Your kid hears a lot of no and stop in a day. Good things she does can pass with no words at all. What you name out loud is what she will repeat.

Tonight's move: Tonight, watch for one good thing she does and name it at once. Say what she did, in plain detail. You put your cup in the sink. That helped us.

Tonight's play: Play a kind spy game at home. Each of you hunts for a good deed to name out loud. She finds one of yours, you find one of hers, and you cheer each find.

Why it works: A named good deed feels like a win she can hold. She will chase that warm feeling again. You train your own eyes too, to see the good first.

Core move: Name one good thing she does, in detail, right when she does it.

Four more moves, in short

Warn before the tidy switch. Hard moments often come at a switch. Warn her two times before play ends. Say, two more minutes, then we tidy. Say it once more at one minute. Then follow through, calm and kind.

Give two good choices inside the tidy. Give her two choices you can live with inside the job. Say, basket or box. Say, blocks first or books first. Then let her pick and honor the pick she makes.

Ask once, then pause. Little kids need time to hear an ask and act on it. Ask one time, then count slow to ten in your head. If she has not moved, say the same words once more, just as plain.

Keep your hands back and let her try. Your kid wants to do hard things on her own. When she works on a hard thing, keep your hands back for a full slow count. Watch close. Cheer the tries, not just the win. Help only if she asks.

Quick question

How do I get my toddler to clean up toys?

Make the job small: pick one song you like, and put toys in one basket till the song ends. Stop when the song stops, even if a few toys stay out. Give her that one real job to finish, thank her for the work by name, and name one good thing she does right when she does it.
